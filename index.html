01 / 09 — law

Mizan — ميزان

A grounded question-answering engine over Jordanian tax and regulatory documents, built as a product for accounting and tax firms. Every answer cites a specific article, page, and dated version of a specific document — or says the sources don't contain one, without ever calling the model. Generated answers pass a verifier before they're shown: citation markers must be in range, every number must appear in a source, and nothing may be phrased as personal advice; calculations live in a separate, clearly labelled section that each office opts into. Scanned laws are ingested through OCR, and multi-tenant isolation is enforced in the database schema and the vector layer rather than trusted to application code — the same Docker image runs hosted or on a client's own server.

02 / 09 — inbox

WhatsApp Inquiry Assistant

An assistant that answers customer questions on WhatsApp from a company's own data — built for real-estate developers and adapted for clinics. Each message is classified by Claude with structured output, the relevant project facts are pulled from the company's website, spreadsheets, or a read-only database, and a reply is drafted in Arabic or English using those facts only. A guard checks every number and date against the source, and no reply goes out until a staff member approves it; bookings, discounts, and unpublished prices skip the draft and go straight to a person. Webhooks are signature-verified and de-duplicated, and multiple clients run as isolated instances behind a router keyed on the receiving phone number, with an end-to-end test proving one client's messages never reach another's database.

03 / 09 — civic

Muyassar — ميسّر

A civic-tech assistant that explains people's rights under published Jordanian laws — starting with personal data protection and the rights of persons with disabilities — in plain Arabic, with every statement linked to the article it comes from. Laws are chunked on article boundaries, each chunk carrying the document, article number, issuing body, date, and source link; retrieval is reranked before Claude writes the answer, and the cited text opens right beside it. A known-answer benchmark measures how often the correct article comes back first and in the top five, and those numbers are shown live on the page instead of being hidden. It explains the text; it doesn't give personal legal advice.

04 / 09 — vision

Arabic OCR — CRNN + CTC ↗

A text-line recognizer for Arabic built entirely from scratch — no pretrained weights, no external OCR engine. A CNN feature extractor feeds a two-layer BiLSTM, trained with CTC loss so no character-level alignment is needed. A synthetic-data generator renders hundreds of thousands of Arabic sentences across random fonts and light augmentation to train it, and a classical ink-projection step segments full scanned pages into individual lines before recognition — sized to train end-to-end on a 4GB laptop GPU.

09 / 09 — hardware

KNN Shape Classifier + Arduino ↗

A real-time computer vision pipeline that closes the loop from camera to physical hardware. Each frame is converted to grayscale, run through Canny edge detection, and flattened into a pixel vector classified by a KNN model trained to tell squares from circles. The prediction is streamed over serial to an Arduino, which lights a green or yellow LED to match — no cloud, no deep learning, just classical CV and a distance metric running live.
